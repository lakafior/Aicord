Discord, native on iPhone and Mac.

Aicord is an unofficial Discord client written in Swift and SwiftUI: system lists and Liquid Glass, encrypted voice and video calls, and Intelligence that runs on your device.

Know before you use it.

Aicord is a fork of Paicord, rebuilt for the newest Apple platforms with almost nothing but Apple's own frameworks. It is honest about what a third-party client is:

  • Unofficial. Aicord is not made by or affiliated with Discord. Using any third-party client is against Discord's Terms of Service, and the risk of an account ban never goes away. Use it at your own risk.
  • Notifications only while it runs. Discord's push notifications reach only the official app, and Aicord will not run a server that keeps your token to forward them. Keep the official app installed if you need to be reached on iPhone.
  • No server of its own. Everything goes straight from your device to Discord.

What it does

Discord as you know it, plus a few things the official app does not do.

Voice and calls

Server voice channels and DM or group calls, end-to-end encrypted with DAVE, with push-to-talk and device choice. Stage channels to listen in, raise a hand and speak.

Video and screen sharing

Cameras and Go Live streams, several side by side or one full screen. Share your own camera, or your screen from iPhone, iPad and Mac.

One search for everything

Servers, channels, people and messages, with date and regex filters. A result opens right at the message.

Intelligence, on device

Translate messages, summarize a channel, catch up on a server or ask a channel a question, with links back to the messages. Read-only, and on your device unless you add an API of your own.

Messages done properly

Formatting from the edit menu or ⌘B, a live preview, slash commands, bot buttons and forms, voice messages, polls, pins, threads and forwards.

Notifications by your rules

Per server and channel, quiet hours and keywords, with Reply and Mark as Read. They follow your Discord settings and need no push server.

Nearly all of Discord's settings

Privacy, content filters, devices, connections, appearance, chat and notifications, saved to your account so the official apps see them too.

Tools for moderators

Timeout, kick and ban with a reason, channels, categories and roles with every permission, AutoMod, emoji and stickers, and the audit log.

Invites that open in the app

Create invite links and preview a server before joining. A Safari extension sends discord.gg links straight to Aicord.

Kept for you

Offline reading, channel export to Markdown or PDF, message reminders, saved messages, and private notes and tags about people.

Nothing slips by

Ghost pings, keyword and person alerts, deleted messages and edit history kept in memory, hidden channels and a local word filter.

Part of the system

Spotlight, an unread and mentions widget, a Live Activity for calls, Shortcuts and Siri, and Handoff between iPhone and Mac.

For your assistant, too

On the Mac, a read-only MCP server lets Claude and other assistants read and search what you allow. Off until you turn it on.

Native, not a web view

System lists and Liquid Glass throughout, Swift 6 with strict concurrency, and six dependencies in all.
